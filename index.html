A supportive place to learn.

A pregnancy scare or an unexpected pregnancy can bring up many feelings. You deserve respect, accurate information, and help from a trusted adult or health professional. This page is informational; it is not a private or confidential care service.

The basics

What should I know?

These are general starting points, not a diagnosis. Every person's situation is different.

1

Pregnancy can happen

Pregnancy may occur when sperm fertilizes an egg. No birth control method is perfect, and having questions is a good reason to talk with a healthcare professional.

2

Symptoms are not proof

A late period, nausea, or tiredness can have many causes. A pregnancy test and advice from a qualified health professional are better ways to find out.

3

Support matters

If someone is pregnant, they deserve non-judgmental medical care and support. They should be able to ask questions and talk through their options without pressure.

If you are worried

Take one step at a time.

If pregnancy might be possible, try to speak with a trusted adult, school nurse, clinic, or doctor. They can help you understand testing, health care, and what choices are available where you live.

  • Choose someone you trust and tell them what is worrying you.
  • Most home tests can be used from the first day of a missed period. If you do not know when your period is due, NHS guidance says to test at least 21 days after unprotected sex.
  • If a test is positive, arrange medical care to discuss next steps.
  • Ask about privacy and confidentiality before sharing personal details.

Looking ahead

Ways to reduce the chance of pregnancy

A healthcare professional or sexual health clinic can explain which options are available and suitable for you. You have the right to ask questions and make decisions without pressure.

Learn about contraception

Methods include condoms, pills, implants, and IUDs. They work in different ways; a clinician can help compare effectiveness, access, and possible side effects.

Know that condoms also help

Condoms can reduce the risk of many sexually transmitted infections as well as pregnancy. Using another contraceptive method with a condom can provide added protection.

Consent is essential

Everyone has the right to say no, change their mind, and set boundaries. Pressure, threats, or force are not consent. Reach out to a trusted person if you feel unsafe.

Common questions

Good questions to ask

Can I get help without telling everyone?

Privacy rules for teens vary by location and by type of care. Before sharing details, ask the clinic or health professional what they keep private and when they may need to involve someone else. This page does not provide confidential care, and any linked website has its own privacy practices.

What if I am not ready to talk to a parent or guardian?

Consider another trusted adult, such as a school counselor, nurse, doctor, relative, or youth worker. If you feel in danger, contact a safe adult or local emergency service.

What if my friend is the one who may be pregnant?

Listen without judgment, ask what support they want, and encourage them to speak with a qualified health professional. Do not share their personal information without permission unless there is an immediate safety concern.

You are not alone

Find a person who can help.

Reach out to a trusted adult, school nurse or counselor, family doctor, or a local youth or sexual health clinic. Ask for accurate information, confidential support, and help understanding your options.

If you are in immediate danger or need urgent medical help, contact your local emergency service or go to an emergency department.
